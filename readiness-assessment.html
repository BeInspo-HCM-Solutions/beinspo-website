Readiness Assessment

Know what you’re getting into before you commit.

A deep dive, not a design. Six to ten weeks on how your organization actually runs, so that what you budget, what you decide early and what you ask a partner for all rest on evidence rather than on a vendor’s estimate.

Book a session What you receive

At a glance

Duration
Six to ten weeks. Closer to six for one pillar, closer to ten for HCM and ERP together.
Who you work with
Two senior practitioners: an HCM or ERP lead and an integration and data lead. The same people present every finding.
Commercial basis
Fixed fee for a fixed scope. A portion is credited against Implementation if you continue with us.
You leave with
A report that is yours to keep, written for budgeting, governance and an RFP, whichever partner you choose.

Who it is for, and who it is not for.

For

  • An organization preparing a business case or budget request for an HCM or ERP replacement.
  • An organization about to issue an RFP that wants the requirements to reflect what it actually needs.
  • An organization that has chosen a platform but not a partner.

Not for

  • An organization that has already signed an implementation contract. Implementation Assurance is the better fit.
  • An organization looking for a solution design or a configuration workbook. We size the work; we don’t design the solution here.

What we look into

Six questions we answer for your organization.

01 / 06

Where does your data go today?

We map every system that talks to HR, Payroll and Finance, including the report run from one system and typed into another, and the file emailed between departments overnight. Those manual flows are what surface as surprise integrations mid-build. We surface them first.

In your reportIntegration inventory

02 / 06

How does the work actually run?

Every process that touches people and pay, walked end to end: hire to retire, each payroll cycle, transfers and multiple assignments, leave, year-end T4, and the handoffs between HR, Payroll and Finance. A by-product is the full list of reports your teams rely on.

In your reportCurrent-state assessment

03 / 06

What will have to change in your data?

Three kinds of finding: what cannot move as it is, what can move but will hurt later, and the decisions that need months of deliberation, such as a chart of accounts or an organization structure. Surfacing those now gives your team months of runway instead of weeks of pressure.

In your reportData-decision registerEarly-decision list

04 / 06

What becomes automatic?

For each process, what is manual today, what is automated, and what the new system would take off your people’s desks. Anchored in your processes, not in anyone’s brochure.

In your reportAutomation baseline

05 / 06

How big is the work, module by module?

Core HR, Absence, Time, Payroll, and Finance where it is in scope. Two questions each: can what you have be accommodated, and roughly how many configurations, custom builds, formulas and reports will it take? Enough to size the implementation without designing it.

In your reportScope and sizing

06 / 06

Which connections do you need on day one?

Which integrations must be live at go-live, which can wait, which manual flows disappear with a process change instead of a build, and which downstream systems need work of their own.

In your reportIntegration inventory

What you get

What you walk away with. Built to be used, whichever partner you choose.

The report

Yours to keep, written for budgeting, governance and an RFP. Eight parts:

  • Current-state assessmentHow HR, payroll, time and finance actually run, with the exceptions and workarounds made visible.
  • Scope and sizingWhat the implementation will take, in phases and effort, so the budget request is grounded.
  • Integration inventoryEvery system that touches the core, what it carries, who owns it, and what it takes to move.
  • Data-decision registerThe decisions about data that must be made early, who owns each, and what it costs to defer them.
  • Early-decision listWhat to decide before a partner is engaged, so the decisions are yours and not the vendor’s.
  • Automation baselineWhat is manual today, what is automated, and what the new system would take off your people’s desks.
  • Risk registerWhat would otherwise surface mid-build as a surprise, with a mitigation for each.
  • RFP-ready requirementsThe findings restated as requirements a partner can price and be held to.

Hover or tap a part to see what it gives you.

The leadership walkthrough

The findings, the recommendation and the business case, walked through with your leadership team.

The working documents

System diagrams, process flows and the data assessment. Yours to keep and reuse.

It pays for itself the first time it catches one of these:

  • A missed integration
  • A payroll requirement nobody wrote down
  • An absence-plan complication
  • A wrong configuration

Catch one, and the assessment has paid for itself. Catch two, and it has saved you money.

What we need from you

Your people’s time, planned with you.

  • A sponsor who can convene HR, payroll, finance and IT.
  • Time from each functional lead during the session weeks, scheduled with you at kickoff around your payroll and fiscal calendar.
  • Read-only access to current systems and recent pay and absence data.
  • A decision-maker for the walkthrough at the end.

What it costs

A fixed fee, agreed before we start.

A fixed fee agreed before we start, sized by the number of pillars and employee groups in scope. If you continue to Implementation with us, a portion of the fee is credited. If you go elsewhere, the report still pays back the first time it catches a surprise.

Where this fits on the path

Stands on its own. Leads anywhere.

If you continue with us, it becomes the first input to Implementation and the credit applies. If you choose another partner, Implementation Assurance picks up the same report and carries it into the build.
