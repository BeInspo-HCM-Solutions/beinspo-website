Solved-problem walkthroughs

Already solved. Here is what that looks like.

Each walkthrough follows the same shape: the problem as a board experiences it, what a template does wrong, what we build, and how we prove it. The technical documentation behind each one is what we show in the first meeting.

A. Teacher pay on the ministry calendar

The problem
Teachers earn over the school year and are paid across twelve months. Mid-year starts, leaves and departures mean the earned and paid amounts diverge, and the difference has to be right at every exit and every year end.
What a template does wrong
Treats teachers as salaried employees on a calendar year, then patches the differences with manual adjustments that nobody can audit.
What we build
Earnings and absence accruals that track earned-to-date against paid-to-date on the ministry calendar, with exit and year-end settlement calculated by the system and visible on the pay slip and the ledger.
How we prove it
Parallel runs across a start, a leave, a return and an exit, reconciled line by line to the current system before go-live.

B. One person, several assignments

The problem
An educational assistant who also drives a bus and coaches after school holds three assignments, in two unions, with different rates, premiums and absence entitlements, and one T4.
What a template does wrong
Forces a primary assignment and treats the others as exceptions, so time, absence and costing for the secondary roles break.
What we build
An assignment and employment-category model where each assignment carries its own union, rate, schedule and absence plan, with time and costing resolved per assignment and consolidated per person for pay and tax.
How we prove it
A test set of real multi-assignment profiles from your data, run through time entry, absence, pay and costing, signed off by payroll and by each union-facing lead.

C. Recovering an overpayment under a collective agreement

The problem
An overpayment is found after the fact. The collective agreement sets how much can be recovered per pay, requires notice, and may require the employee’s agreement to a schedule.
What a template does wrong
Deducts the full amount on the next pay, or leaves the recovery to a spreadsheet outside the system.
What we build
A recovery process with the negotiated step built in: notice generated, schedule agreed and recorded, deductions capped per the agreement, balance visible to payroll and to the employee, full audit trail.
How we prove it
Walkthrough with payroll and labour relations against the agreement text, then scenario tests for each employee group’s rules.
